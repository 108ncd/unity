Theme · What is bhakti?

Love is what the soul is made of

Bhakti is not one religion among many, or a mood that comes and goes. In the Vedic understanding it is sanātana-dharma: the eternal function of the soul, which is loving service.

Who we are

The self that does not die

The Gītā begins its teaching by turning our attention away from the body to the one who lives in it. The body changes from childhood to youth to old age, and finally it is given up. The self who witnesses all of this does not change.

न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन् na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin “For the soul there is neither birth nor death at any time. He has not come into being, does not come into being, and will not come into being. He is unborn, eternal, ever-existing and primeval. He is not slain when the body is slain.” Bhagavad-gītā 2.20, trans. Prabhupāda

The Gītā then says where the soul comes from. It is a part of God, the way a ray belongs to the sun.

ममैवांशो जीवलोके जीवभूतः सनातनः mamaivāṁśo jīva-loke jīva-bhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ “The living entities in this conditioned world are My eternal fragmental parts.” Bhagavad-gītā 15.7, trans. Prabhupāda

Sanātana-dharma

The function you cannot take away

In everyday speech, “dharma” is often translated as “religion,” something a person can adopt or abandon. Its deeper meaning is the quality that cannot be separated from a thing. Heat is the dharma of fire. Wetness is the dharma of water. Take the heat away and it is no longer fire. Prabhupāda draws out this meaning in the introduction to his Bhagavad-gītā.

So what is the soul's dharma? Look at any life and you will see the answer. Everyone serves. A parent serves a child, a worker serves an employer, a citizen serves a country, and even a person who lives only for pleasure serves their own senses. No one can stop serving. We can only choose whom we serve.

jīvera ‘svarūpa’ haya — kṛṣṇera ‘nitya-dāsa’ “It is the living entity's constitutional position to be an eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa.” Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya-līlā 20.108, trans. Prabhupāda

When that service is given to the Source of all, with love, it is bhakti. It is not something added to the soul from outside. It is the soul acting as it truly is. Every other dharma, whether family duty, social duty or religious law, finds its fulfilment here. That is why we call the other paths stepping stones: each one trains the heart to serve, and bhakti is where that service becomes love.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.1.11

What makes service pure

Not every act of service is bhakti in its highest sense. Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, a direct follower of Śrī Caitanya, gave the classic definition:

anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyaṁ jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam / ānukūlyena kṛṣṇānu-śīlanaṁ bhaktir uttamā “One should render transcendental loving service to the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa favorably and without desire for material profit or gain through fruitive activities or philosophical speculation. That is called pure devotional service.” Quoted in Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya-līlā 19.167, trans. Prabhupāda

  1. Free of other desires anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam

    It is not a way to get something else, whether wealth, fame or even liberation. Love is its own goal.

  2. Not covered by karma or jñāna jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam

    Work and knowledge serve it, but it does not depend on them. A simple heart can reach what a scholar might miss.

  3. Favourable ānukūlyena

    It is done to please the beloved, in the way the beloved wants, not in the way that suits us.

The Nārada Pañcarātra, also quoted in the Caitanya-caritāmṛta (Madhya 19.170), adds what bhakti looks like in practice: “engaging all our senses in the service of the Lord, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the master of all the senses.” Nothing has to be suppressed. Everything is given a new owner.
