Spiritual Unity Movement

Many rivers, one ocean of love

Every sincere tradition carries the soul somewhere true. We see all of them flowing toward the same place: bhakti, pure loving devotion to the Divine, which we hold to be the soul's highest expression.

एकं सद्विप्रा बहुधा वदन्ति ekaṁ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti “Truth is one; the wise call it by many names.” Ṛg Veda 1.164.46

Our perspective

Stepping stones, not rivals

We do not ask anyone to leave their tradition. We believe each dharma, each school of thought, and each honest search for truth is a real step on the soul's journey. Right action purifies the heart. Knowledge clears away illusion. Meditation steadies the mind. And when the heart is clear, what naturally rises is love: love for the Source of all, and through that, love for every living being.

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa puts it this way, and it is the conviction at the centre of this site:

स वै पुंसां परो धर्मो यतो भक्तिरधोक्षजे । अहैतुक्यप्रतिहता ययात्मा सुप्रसीदति ॥ “The supreme occupation (dharma) for all humanity is that by which men can attain to loving devotional service unto the transcendent Lord. Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self.” Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.2.6, trans. A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

The ascent the Gītā describes

How the paths build on one another

The Bhagavad-gītā names several yogas. Read together, they form a ladder, and the Gītā itself names the top rung (6.47): of all yogīs, the one who worships Kṛṣṇa with faith, absorbed in Him, is the most intimately united.

  1. Karma-yoga action as offering

    Doing one's duty honestly, without clinging to the fruit. This is where most religious law and ethics live: the commandments, the sharia, the precepts, the rule of life.

  2. Jñāna-yoga knowledge

    Understanding that the self is not the body. Philosophy, theology and scripture study belong here, as do the questions science asks about what is real.

  3. Dhyāna-yoga meditation

    Quieting the mind and turning attention inward. The contemplative traditions of every faith, from Zen to the Desert Fathers, carry this practice.

  4. Bhakti-yoga loving devotion

    Action, knowledge and meditation offered in love to a personal Divine. Here the practices of every rung are kept, and their purpose is fulfilled.

Unity in diversity

What each tradition carries

These are short sketches written with respect, not summaries any tradition would sign off on. Each one has its own depth and its own self-understanding, and we honour that difference.

Sanātana Dharma

Emphasis

Dharma, the cycle of rebirth, and many paths to one Absolute.

Where love shines

The bhakti saints: Caitanya, Mīrābāī, Tulsīdās, the Āḻvārs and Nāyanārs.

Judaism

Emphasis

Covenant, Torah, and the oneness of God.

Where love shines

The Shema: love God with all your heart, soul and might.

Christianity

Emphasis

Grace, incarnation, and the life of Christ.

Where love shines

“God is love,” and the two great commandments.

Islam

Emphasis

Tawḥīd, surrender (islām) to the One God, and prophetic guidance.

Where love shines

The Sufi path of ʿishq, divine love, from Rābiʿa onward.

Sikhism

Emphasis

One Creator, remembrance of the Name, honest work and sharing.

Where love shines

Prem and bhagti throughout the Gurū Granth Sāhib.

Buddhism

Emphasis

The end of suffering through insight and ethical living.

Where love shines

Mettā and karuṇā: boundless loving-kindness and compassion.

Jainism

Emphasis

Ahiṁsā, self-discipline, and the liberation of the soul.

Where love shines

Reverence for every living being, and anekāntavāda: truth seen from many sides.

Taoism

Emphasis

Harmony with the Tao, simplicity, and effortless action.

Where love shines

Compassion, the first of Laozi's “three treasures.”

Philosophy and science

Emphasis

Honest inquiry into what is real and how we know it.

Where love shines

The love of wisdom itself: philo-sophia.
