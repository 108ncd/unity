Themes · The bridge

The power of the mind

Nobody leaps from “only matter is real” straight to the eternal soul. There has to be a bridge, and the bridge is something we already live with every day: the mind. Once we see that the mind is a real, subtle energy that shapes the body and the world around it, the step to the soul is no longer a leap of faith. It is simply the next level of the same reality.

Where all spirituality begins

We are beings of energy

Mystics, yogīs, occultists and esoteric schools all over the world use different words, but they share one starting point. A human being is not only a body. We are layered beings of energy, and the body is only the outermost and densest layer.

The Taittirīya Upaniṣad describes five such layers, or kośas (sheaths), each one finer than the last and each one held inside the next.

tasmād vā etasmāt prāṇamayāt anyo ’ntara ātmā manomayaḥ “Different from this self made of breath, and within it, is a self made of mind.” Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.3 (our rendering)

Of the subtle layers, the mind is the one we can observe most directly. Strictly, the breath sheath sits between body and mind, linking the two, which is why the breath changes the moment our mood does. But the mind is the first subtle level we can watch, test and work with in daily life. That makes it the natural place to begin exploring.

Down-to-earth evidence

The mind shapes the body

We do not need to start with anything exotic. Mainstream science has already documented, again and again, that what happens in the mind changes what happens in the body. These findings are well established and widely replicated.

  • Belief that healsThe placebo effect is real enough that every new drug must beat it in trials. In 1978 researchers showed that placebo pain relief can be blocked by naloxone, a drug that blocks the body's own opioids. The expectation of relief was making the brain release its own painkillers (Levine, Gordon and Fields, The Lancet, 1978). In a 2010 Harvard study, patients with irritable bowel syndrome improved even when they were told honestly that their pills contained no medicine (Kaptchuk et al., PLOS ONE).
  • Belief that harmsThe reverse, the nocebo effect, is just as real. Patients warned about a side effect report it far more often, even when they were given a sugar pill.
  • Practice without movingVolunteers who only imagined practising a piano exercise for five days showed changes in the motor areas of the brain much like those who really practised (Pascual-Leone et al., Journal of Neurophysiology, 1995). In another study, imagined muscle training increased finger strength by about 22 per cent, against 30 per cent for physical training (Yue and Cole, Journal of Neurophysiology, 1992).
  • Meditation and heatHarvard cardiologist Herbert Benson measured Tibetan monks practising g-tummo meditation. They raised the temperature of their fingers and toes by up to about 8°C by mental technique alone (Benson et al., Nature, 1982).
  • Mind and immunityVolunteers trained in breathing and meditation techniques could deliberately damp down their immune system's inflammatory response to an injected bacterial toxin, something long thought to be beyond voluntary control (Kox et al., PNAS, 2014).
  • A brain reshapedLong-term meditators show measurable differences in brain structure in regions linked to attention and self-awareness (Lazar et al., NeuroReport, 2005). The brain is reshaped by how we use the mind.

None of this proves the soul. But it quietly overturns the picture of the mind as a passive by-product of the body. Thought, belief and attention act on the physical. They are causes, not only effects.

“For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he.” Proverbs 23:7, King James Version

Beyond the skin

The mind reaches others

Our mind does not stop at the edge of our body. Moods and states move between people in ways that science is only beginning to measure.

  • Emotional contagionWe unconsciously mirror the faces, voices and postures of the people around us, and their feelings arrive in us with them. Psychologists call this emotional contagion (Hatfield, Cacioppo and Rapson, Emotional Contagion, 1994).
  • Happiness spreadsA twenty-year study of nearly 5,000 people found that happiness spreads through social networks, up to three degrees of separation: to the friends of our friends' friends (Fowler and Christakis, BMJ, 2008).
  • Hearts in stepAt a fire-walking ritual in Spain, the heart rhythms of spectators who were close to a walker rose and fell in step with the walker's own (Konvalinka et al., PNAS, 2011).

These are still ordinary channels: the senses, the nervous system, the body. Yet they show something the traditions have always said. We live inside a shared field of mind, and what we carry inwardly affects everyone around us. Prayer, kīrtan and group meditation all rest on this truth.

The mystical frontier

When scientists studied the paranormal

Some reports go further: of the mind acting at a distance, perceiving without the senses, or moving matter directly. Here we move from settled science to open and contested questions. It is worth knowing that some of the greatest scientists of the modern age took these questions seriously enough to test them.

The Curies and the medium

Between 1905 and 1908 the Institut Général Psychologique in Paris held more than forty séances with the Italian medium Eusapia Palladino. Pierre and Marie Curie both attended, treating the sittings as scientific experiments. Others in the room included the physiologist Charles Richet, who later won the Nobel Prize, the philosopher Henri Bergson, and the physicists Paul Langevin and Jean Perrin. Pierre Curie, who had discovered radioactivity with Marie, wondered whether these phenomena might point to an unknown form of energy.

For honesty: Palladino was caught cheating on other occasions, freeing a hand or foot to move objects in the dark. The Paris sittings were in darkness, and fraud cannot be ruled out. Marie Curie wrote that some phenomena had “seemed very convincing”, but her biographers describe her as less taken with them than Pierre was.

Psychical research

The Curies were not alone. The Society for Psychical Research was founded in London in 1882 by Cambridge scholars to investigate these questions with scientific method. The chemist Sir William Crookes, the physicist Sir Oliver Lodge and the psychologist William James all took part in such research. James spent years studying the Boston medium Leonora Piper and came away convinced that she knew things she had no ordinary way of knowing.

The research continues

Laboratory parapsychology is still active, and still disputed. Meta-analyses of “ganzfeld” telepathy experiments report results above chance (Storm, Tressoldi and Di Risio, Psychological Bulletin, 2010), while critics argue that weaknesses in method and selective reporting could explain them (Hyman, in the same journal, 2010). The Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research lab studied whether intention could influence random machines from 1979 to 2007, but a large replication by other labs did not confirm its main result. We present these as open questions, not proofs.

A Vedic reading of the evidence

In Human Devolution: A Vedic Alternative to Darwin's Theory (Torchlight Publishing, 2003), the researcher Michael A. Cremo (Drutakarma Dāsa) gathers this whole body of reports, from the early psychical researchers to modern studies of past-life memories and out-of-body experiences. He reads it through the Vedic picture: a human being is made of three things, gross matter, a subtle but still material mind, and conscious spirit. Instead of consciousness evolving up out of matter, he argues, the conscious self has come down into matter, and can rise again through prayer, meditation and yoga.

Cremo writes openly as a member of a Vaiṣṇava tradition, and mainstream scientists reject his wider claims about the origin and age of humanity. We include his book because it assembles the historical record on mind and spirit in one place and frames it in the same layered model as the kośas, not because every claim in it is settled.

Mind over matter

Subtle energy shapes the gross

The Vedic sources explain all this through one principle: the subtler energy governs the grosser. Mind is finer than the body, so it moves the body, and through the body, the world. Our thoughts become words, our words become actions, our actions become circumstances. Over time, the mind's imprints become the situations of our life. The site's pages on karma and psychology explore this in depth.

bandhur ātmātmanas tasya yenātmaivātmanā jitaḥ / anātmanas tu śatrutve vartetātmaiva śatru-vat “For one who has mastered the mind, the mind is the best of friends. For one who has not, the same mind acts like an enemy.” Bhagavad-gītā 6.6 (our rendering)

This is why every spiritual tradition begins with the mind. Prayer, mantra, meditation, fasting, examination of conscience and mindfulness are all ways of mastering this subtle energy, so that it serves us instead of ruling us.

The next dimension

From the mind to the soul

Once we accept the mind as a real subtle energy, the soul is not a jump into the unknown. It is the next layer inward. The Gītā describes this ladder in a single verse:

indriyāṇi parāṇy āhur indriyebhyaḥ paraṁ manaḥ / manasas tu parā buddhir yo buddheḥ paratas tu saḥ “The senses are higher than the body, the mind is higher than the senses, intelligence is higher than the mind, and the self is higher still than intelligence.” Bhagavad-gītā 3.42 (our rendering)

Two lines of research point toward this deeper level, and both remain debated. Psychiatrist Ian Stevenson at the University of Virginia spent forty years documenting children who seemed to remember previous lives, sometimes with details that could be checked (Twenty Cases Suggestive of Reincarnation, 1966). And in a Dutch study of 344 cardiac-arrest survivors, 18 per cent reported near-death experiences, some vivid and lucid at a time when their brains were severely compromised (van Lommel et al., The Lancet, 2001). Researchers disagree about how to explain both.

What the traditions add is the experience of those who have gone further inward. The mind connects us to others through a shared field. The soul connects us more deeply still, because on that level we are not separate at all. Each soul is a spark of the same Source.

What unites us

One bridge, many traditions

Here is where the traditions meet. Hindu and yogic schools speak of the kośas and the ātmā. Buddhists speak of training the mind until its luminous nature shines. Jews, Christians and Muslims speak of the heart and the soul breathed into us by God. Taoists cultivate qi and stillness. Mystics in every culture describe the same journey: from the body, through the mind, to a self that is deeper than both.

They disagree about many things. But they agree on this: consciousness is not merely a product of matter, and the inner world is real. That shared ground is the first step from believing in nothing to a spiritual life. From there, the path continues to the four levels of awareness and finally to bhakti, the soul's love for its Source.
