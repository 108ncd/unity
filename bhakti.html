Bhakti

The one language every tradition speaks

Prophets, saints and scriptures disagree about many things. On love for God they sound remarkably alike. New to the idea? Start with What is bhakti?

सा त्वस्मिन् परमप्रेमरूपा sā tv asmin parama-prema-rūpā “Bhakti is of the nature of supreme love for Him.” Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 2

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 7.5.23

The nine practices of devotion

Prahlāda, the child-devotee of the Bhāgavatam, lists nine ways love for God is lived. Look closely and you will find each of them in every tradition on this page.

  • ŚravaṇamHearing about God
  • KīrtanamSinging God's names and glories
  • SmaraṇamRemembering God
  • Pāda-sevanamServing God's feet
  • ArcanamWorship
  • VandanamPrayer and bowing down
  • DāsyamServing as a servant
  • SakhyamFriendship with God
  • Ātma-nivedanamOffering one's whole self

Universal bhakti

Everyone already has bhakti

A devotee does not divide the world into those who love God and those who do not. Bhakti is the soul's own nature, so it is present in every living being, whatever their tradition, their philosophy, or the object of their love. What differs is only where it is directed.

Bhakti can flow straight to the Source. It can also flow to the Source's manifestations: a mother's devotion to her child, a scientist's devotion to truth, an artist's devotion to beauty, a patriot's to their country, an activist's to justice. All of these are the same love, reaching for something that carries a spark of the Divine.

यद्यद्विभूतिमत्सत्त्वं श्रीमदूर्जितमेव वा yad yad vibhūtimat sattvaṁ śrīmad ūrjitam eva vā / tat tad evāvagaccha tvaṁ mama tejo-’ṁśa-sambhavam “Whatever is glorious, beautiful or powerful, know that it springs from a spark of My splendour.” Bhagavad-gītā 10.41 (our rendering)

The Gītā goes further. Whatever form people choose to worship, the Divine strengthens their faith (7.21), and every path people take is in the end a path toward the One:

ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāṁs tathaiva bhajāmy aham / mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ “As people approach Me, so I respond to them. Everyone, in every way, is following My path.” Bhagavad-gītā 4.11 (our rendering)

The devotee's way of seeing

The Bhāgavatam describes the most advanced devotee not by how much they pray, but by how they see others. They recognise in everyone the same love for God that lives in their own heart.

sarva-bhūteṣu yaḥ paśyed bhagavad-bhāvam ātmanaḥ / bhūtāni bhagavaty ātmany eṣa bhāgavatottamaḥ “One who sees in every being the same love for God that is in their own heart, and sees all beings resting in God, is the highest devotee.” Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.2.45 (our rendering)

This is the heart of unity in diversity. We do not need to agree on names or doctrines to recognise the same love at work in each other.
