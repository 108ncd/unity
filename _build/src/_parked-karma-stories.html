Stories from practice

When the inner state changes, life changes

Lazarev's books are full of accounts from his consultations. In each one the outer problem, whether illness, money, a relationship or an accident, turns out to have an inner root: usually an attachment to something earthly that has grown stronger than love. Here are five, retold briefly.

Destiny

The driver's golden rule

When Lazarev began driving, he noticed that his emotions and his troubles on the road were linked. Cursing a pedestrian in his mind could end in an accident; contempt for a traffic officer could bring an unfair fine. So he made himself a rule: respect pedestrians, other drivers, the traffic police and the road signs. Driving became easier and his mishaps lighter. Later he added a fifth point: respect the road itself, whatever its state.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 4: Contact with the Future, pp. 15–16

Finances

The company that burned

A bookkeeper told Lazarev that their company had literally burned down and gone bankrupt. Looking at the field of the firm and its director, he saw the cause in the director's growing attachment to money, which had turned into hostility toward people. That aggression spread through the whole staff until the company became, in his words, “an aggressive organism,” and it destroyed itself.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 285–286

Relationships

Forgiving is not enough

A woman who could not have children was told she would first have to let go of every grievance against her husband. She returned saying she had forgiven everything. Lazarev saw that only half the work was done. Forgiving was not enough, he told her: she also had to accept what had happened, thank God for it as a cleansing of her soul, and feel love toward everything that comes. To his surprise, she managed it.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 198–199

Kindness

The employee he took back

A small business owner in New York had supported a young man out of pity, paying him even when there was no work. The young man left for a better job, then quickly lost it. The owner could have had him sent away, but held back, and instead phoned him to offer his job back. Lazarev saw this as a man whose own field had been cleansed by choosing kindness over resentment.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 234–235

Health

The sisters who changed

In 1994 a young woman came to Lazarev in serious condition after several operations. They talked for about an hour about her attitude to life. Six months later she returned well, and he reports that her field was now clear. What struck her most was something else: her two sisters had become noticeably kinder, though she had told them nothing. Her inner change had reached them through the field.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, p. 115

These are Lazarev's own accounts and interpretations, retold in our words. They describe a spiritual view of illness and destiny, not medical advice. Anyone facing illness should keep working with their doctors. Inner change and good medical care belong together.
