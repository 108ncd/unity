Theme · Gradual awareness

Four levels of awareness

Self-realisation unfolds in stages. First we see that we are not our patterns. Then we discover that our awareness is eternal. Then we find the presence of the Divine within. Finally we meet the Divine as a Person to be loved. Each level is real, and each one prepares the next.

ब्रह्मेति परमात्मेति भगवानिति शब्द्यते vadanti tat tattva-vidas tattvaṁ yaj jñānam advayam / brahmeti paramātmeti bhagavān iti śabdyate “Those who know the Truth say that the one non-dual Reality is called Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān.” Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.2.11 (our rendering)

Level 1 · Body, mind and awareness

I am not my patterns

The first step is to tell apart three things we usually mix together. There is the body, which we can see and touch. There is the mind, with its thoughts, emotions, memories and habits. And there is awareness, the one who notices both.

Buddhism offers a precise method here. It divides experience into five skandhas or aggregates: form, feeling, perception, mental formations and consciousness. Then it looks at each one honestly and asks whether it is really “me.”

The Vedic tradition draws a similar map. The Gītā (7.4) lists eight elements of material nature: earth, water, fire, air and ether make up the gross body, and mind, intelligence and false ego make up the subtle body. The self is none of them.

Where imprints live

Our experiences leave imprints, called saṁskāras and vāsanās. They are stored on several levels at once:

  • In the brainAs memories, beliefs and the grooves of habit.
  • In the bodyAs tension, posture and reflexes. Modern trauma research describes how the body holds on to what the mind has forgotten.
  • In the subtle bodyAs deeper tendencies that the Vedic tradition says can even carry from one life to the next.

Some of these patterns we create ourselves. Many we absorb from family, culture and environment. The more we identify with them, the less aware we are. The more we can watch them without becoming them, the freer we become.

yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ / tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe ’vasthānam “Yoga is the stilling of the movements of the mind. Then the seer rests in its own nature.” Yoga Sūtras 1.2–1.3 (our rendering)

Level 2 · Brahman · sat

Awareness is eternal

Once we see that we are awareness, the next question is: what is awareness? The Upaniṣads answer that it does not begin with the body or end with it. It is spirit, unchanging and eternal, and it shares its nature with the infinite spirit behind everything, called Brahman.

tat tvam asi “You are That.” Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7

At this level the soul tastes sat, eternal being. It is a deep peace: freedom from fear of death, and from the restless sense that we are only a body in a passing world.

Level 3 · Paramātmā · sat-cit

The friend within

Going deeper, the soul finds it is not alone in its awareness. Within the heart there is a greater Consciousness, the Paramātmā or Supersoul, who witnesses and guides. Here love begins, quiet and meditative, as the individual consciousness takes shelter in its link with the Supreme.

īśvaraḥ sarva-bhūtānāṁ hṛd-deśe ’rjuna tiṣṭhati “The Lord dwells in the heart of every being, Arjuna.” Bhagavad-gītā 18.61 (our rendering)

This is the path of the yogīs who meditate on the Lord in the heart. At this level the soul tastes sat and cit: eternal being, and living knowledge of the One who is always with it.

Level 4 · Bhagavān · sat-cit-ānanda

The Beloved with a name and a face

At the summit, the Supreme is known in full, as Bhagavān, the Person who has everything that makes a person lovable. Here the Divine is not only being and consciousness but also joy, and joy needs a relationship.

  • NāmaNames that can be called and sung
  • GuṇaQualities such as kindness, beauty and humour
  • RūpaA form that can be seen with the eyes of love
  • LīlāActivities and pastimes shared with those who love
īśvaraḥ paramaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ sac-cid-ānanda-vigrahaḥ “The supreme controller is [the all-attractive Personality of God], whose form is eternity, knowledge and bliss.” Brahma-saṁhitā 5.1 (our rendering)
raso vai saḥ “He is sweetness itself. Receiving that sweetness, one becomes full of joy.” Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.7 (our rendering)

At this level the soul tastes sat-cit-ānanda: eternity, knowledge and bliss together. This is bhakti in its fullness.

Unity in diversity

Where the traditions place their emphasis

Every tradition touches all four levels somewhere in its depths. Most of them put their main emphasis on one. Seen this way, they do not contradict each other. They describe the same Reality in more or less detail.

These are emphases, not boxes. No tradition is limited to one level, and many people within each one reach far beyond it.

Grounded progress

No skipping steps

The levels build on each other. Love for God as a Person needs the steadiness of the earlier stages beneath it. If we still identify completely with our body and our moods, it is easy to mistake emotional excitement for advanced devotion. Real progress shows up as more awareness, more peace, more kindness and less reactivity, on every level at once.

So we cannot honestly claim to be advancing in love of God while neglecting the basics: seeing our patterns, living ethically, and growing in self-awareness. In this sense the levels do not compete. Each one protects and completes the others, and everything is harmonised.

Awareness and karma

Climbing out of the cycle

Karma rests on identification. As long as we take ourselves to be the body and mind, our imprints drive our actions and our actions return as situations. Each level of awareness loosens that chain a little more. At the first level we stop being our patterns. At the second we stop fearing loss. At the third we are guided from within. At the fourth, love does what effort could not, and the soul lives in this world free from its reactions. The yoga tradition calls this kaivalya.
