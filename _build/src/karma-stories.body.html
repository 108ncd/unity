When the inner state changes, life changes

These accounts come from Sergey Lazarev's Diagnostics of Karma books. In each one the outer problem, whether illness, money, a relationship or an accident, turns out to have an inner root: usually an attachment to something earthly that has grown stronger than love. Here are twenty-five, five for each area of life, retold briefly in our own words.

Destiny

The driver's golden rule

When Lazarev began driving, he noticed that his emotions and his troubles on the road were linked. Cursing a pedestrian in his mind could end in an accident; contempt for a traffic officer could bring an unfair fine. So he made himself a rule: respect pedestrians, other drivers, the traffic police and the road signs. Driving became easier and his mishaps lighter. Later he added a fifth point: respect the road itself, whatever its state.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 4: Contact with the Future, pp. 15–16

The crash without a scratch

A young man came to Lazarev while his mother was dying. Lazarev told him a hard thing: by his reading, it was the son's life that had been in danger, and his mother was unconsciously shielding him. The young man began to pray. A few days later he phoned: he and his fiancée had been in a car accident, the car was destroyed, and neither had a scratch. Lazarev's view was that the danger had been real, and that prayer had brought it forward and softened it. Because the prayer held more love than self-interest, he said, help from above was allowed to reach him.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 4: Contact with the Future, pp. 109–110

The dream car

A friend offered Lazarev a brand-new car for almost nothing. Its owner, a sailor, had dreamed of a car for years. Each time he sat at the wheel he felt struck by an unseen blow, while a neighbour drove it without trouble, and he heard inwardly that he must sell it for that price. Lazarev read it this way: the man had longed for the car so much that owning it had become a mortal danger to him. Losing the car was, in his words, the mildest form of cleansing, though a strange one.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 252–253

The red light

Lazarev and a friend were driving through St Petersburg, grumbling about everything wrong with the country. As they pulled away on a green light, a car ran the red and smashed into theirs. Half a second earlier, Lazarev reckoned, it would have hit exactly where he was sitting. Instead of getting angry, he started to laugh. He had overcome his grievances against his country on the surface, he said, but not in the hidden corners of his soul. The crash was a reminder from above that things were not so bad, and could have been much worse.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 6: Steps toward the Divine (Romanian edition), ch. “Purification”

The road called life

On an evening highway near Haifa, a slow car blocked the fast lane and ignored every signal. Lazarev's companion grew furious. “Let it go,” Lazarev told him, “health is worth more,” and they passed on the right. Others honked and cut in, and the driver only dug in further. In her field Lazarev saw pride and the certainty that she alone was right, and he sensed that her anger at the “unfair” drivers would come back to her later. His conclusion: someone who keeps love and kindness in the soul drives well by instinct, even without knowing the rules. Love lets us survive on the highway called life.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 3: Love (Romanian edition), ch. “The new structures”

Finances

The company that burned

A bookkeeper told Lazarev that their company had literally burned down and gone bankrupt. Looking at the field of the firm and its director, he saw the cause in the director's growing attachment to money, which had turned into hostility toward people. That aggression spread through the whole staff until the company became, in his words, “an aggressive organism,” and it destroyed itself.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 285–286

The jewels that came back

A friend had been given jewellery worth over a thousand dollars to sell. The young men he passed it to vanished, and he was in despair. Lazarev saw a strong inherited attachment to possessions, with resentment toward anyone who took them. His advice: look back over your whole life, thank God for every time you lost something, and ask forgiveness for the bitterness you felt each time. A few days later the friend reported that the jewels had been returned. The young men told him, “At first we wanted to cheat you, then we felt sorry for you,” and could not say why.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 250–251

The healer's own attachment

A friend deep in debt asked Lazarev for help. He saw that she was strongly attached to money and to a prosperous life. To help her, he felt he had to limit his own comfort and income. Outwardly he agreed calmly; inwardly it grated. That friction showed him the same attachment buried in himself. His lesson: when someone asks you for help, it is not by chance. In giving up material wealth, you receive spiritual wealth.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 133–134

Saving the soul, not the firm

A patient whose small firm was close to bankruptcy brushed aside talk of inner work. He only wanted to know how to save his finances. Lazarev answered that he cared more about saving the man's soul. For this man, success with money had become proof of his own perfection, and his anxiety was putting him in real danger. Money has to be earned, Lazarev told him, but not thought about all the time. Right now you are being healed through a lack of money. Accept it, or the lesson will come in a harder form. The patient said he understood, and Lazarev saw in his field that he truly had.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 3: Love (Romanian edition), ch. “The new structures”

The loan

As a favour, Lazarev arranged a large money transfer for an acquaintance, who then failed to repay it, month after month. Lazarev promised the lender he would cover it himself if he had to. What pleased him most, he wrote, was that he felt no resentment or hatred toward the man: for that alone it is worth losing any sum. The money was finally returned, then stolen the next day from the relative carrying it. Soon after, the 1998 crash swept away his businesses. He took it all as a cure and grew completely calm, turning his longing inward, toward God, instead of outward.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 6: Steps toward the Divine (Romanian edition), ch. “Personality”

Relationships

Forgiving is not enough

A woman who could not have children was told she would first have to let go of every grievance against her husband. She returned saying she had forgiven everything. Lazarev saw that only half the work was done. Forgiving was not enough, he told her: she also had to accept what had happened, thank God for it as a cleansing of her soul, and feel love toward everything that comes. To his surprise, she managed it.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 198–199

The marriage her mother opposed

A woman's field showed the marks of an unintended curse from her mother, who had fiercely opposed her marriage. Yet it had done her no harm. Lazarev's reading went back a life: then, the same mother had made her give up the same man, whom she loved. This time she was given the same test and chose love, and so her mother's anger could not touch her. The mother, he said, had failed her own repeated test by setting herself against her daughter's love.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 1: Towards a Medicine of the Soul (French edition), PDF p. 91

Principles or love

A doctor in New York could not understand why he had fallen ill. He ate well and held no hatred toward anyone. Asked whether he had felt constant irritation toward someone, he admitted it: for over a year, toward his own son. Lazarev told him the son had been given to him precisely because he contradicted his principles. You may disagree, he said, but never give up the feeling of love. When principles matter more than love, trouble follows, so do not turn away from the people you love.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 4: Contact with the Future, pp. 47–48

Marry your friend

A young couple's marriage was falling apart after a friend of the wife told the husband, out of the blue, that his wife was unfaithful. Lazarev saw that the two had become so attached to each other that even the thought of losing the bond filled them with fear. He asked them to drop every grievance against each other and to pray for their future children, and he told the husband to let the suspicion go completely. He also passed on a friend's father's advice: marry a woman who is your friend. When passion turns into friendship, the family stands even when passion wavers.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 5: Answers to Questions (Romanian edition)

Two questions

A woman told Lazarev that her husband earned nothing, treated her badly and often left, and that life with him had lost all meaning. He asked her two questions. “Are you bored with him?” After a pause: “No.” “Are you drawn to him?” After a longer, painful pause: “Yes.” “That's all,” he said. “You can go.” For a moment she stood there puzzled. Then her face cleared. She smiled, thanked him and left.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 5: Answers to Questions (Romanian edition)

Kindness

The employee he took back

A small business owner in New York had supported a young man out of pity, paying him even when there was no work. The young man left for a better job, then quickly lost it. The owner could have had him sent away, but held back, and instead phoned him to offer his job back. Lazarev saw this as a man whose own field had been cleansed by choosing kindness over resentment.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 234–235

From tank to wedding car

Lazarev's assistant described a woman who stormed in for her appointment “like a tank,” insisting she would go in at exactly ten o'clock. Three hours later she said “excuse me” and smiled. Her mind was as sharp as before, but her eyes had filled with kindness. In the assistant's words, she was no longer a tank but a light car, decorated with flowers for a wedding.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 4: Contact with the Future, pp. 108–109

How to truly wish someone well

A patient shared the prayer she used to help others: “God, send all my love and goodwill to this person's spirit and soul.” It sounded beautiful, but Lazarev saw a hidden flaw. Clinging to another soul, even with love, binds us to it and puts that person in God's place. If you want to help someone, he said, pray that they may be given to love God more than any earthly happiness.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, p. 69

A gift to the one who hurt him

A patient had been offended by someone and could not forgive him, however hard he tried. The answer came unexpectedly. He picked out the things he liked most among his own belongings and gave them to the man who had hurt him. After that, his resentment was gone.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 5: Answers to Questions (Romanian edition)

Find one good thing

Sitting in a steam bath with a friend, Lazarev was still seething: once again, someone he had helped had cheated him. He kept telling himself it was sent from above, but the grievance would not go. His friend smiled. “You teach others not to judge. So what are you doing now?” Lazarev said he was only assessing the facts. “Then find something good in him,” said the friend, “even one thing. It will help.” He tried, and the urge to judge eased. It became his first advice to anyone who has been hurt: look for something to approve of in the person who hurt you.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 5: Answers to Questions (Romanian edition)

Health

The sisters who changed

In 1994 a young woman came to Lazarev in serious condition after several operations. They talked for about an hour about her attitude to life. Six months later she returned well, and he reports that her field was now clear. What struck her most was something else: her two sisters had become noticeably kinder, though she had told them nothing. Her inner change had reached them through the field.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, p. 115

The lecture that helped

A woman awaiting a decision on surgery for a fibroid asked for help. Lazarev sat her down to watch a recording of one of his lectures. Two hours later he found her field much improved, and took it as a sign that the understanding itself does the work. He saw the root in a family habit of feeling superior to others. He asked her to go back over each stage of her life and change how she felt about it, and to pray for her daughters and grandchildren ten times more than for herself.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 2: Pure Karma, pp. 235–236

The boy's sore leg

A six-year-old boy came to Lazarev complaining that his leg hurt. Lazarev asked him to put his hands on his knees and ask forgiveness on behalf of his father, who at eighteen had lost the will to live. The boy did so, and Lazarev reports that he saw the boy's field restored.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 1: Towards a Medicine of the Soul (French edition), PDF p. 147

The footballers' visits

In Israel, Lazarev was told of a little boy with cancer whom the doctors could no longer help. The boy asked his mother to help him meet his favourite footballer. The player came and talked with him, then brought the whole team, and they kept visiting. The boy grew stronger. Later the players took him along to their matches, and in the end he recovered. In Lazarev's words, joy and love did what no specialist could.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 5: Answers to Questions (Romanian edition)

“I love You anyway”

A woman Lazarev knew began to pray, again and again, “Lord, I love You more than anything in the world.” Every time, a fierce pain shot through her head. This went on for two months. Then one day she prayed, “Lord, I love You more than anything in the world anyway, only please don't give me such pain.” The illness left her and never came back.

S. N. Lazarev, Diagnostics of Karma, Book 3: Love (Romanian edition), ch. “Love for people and the world”

These are Lazarev's own accounts and interpretations, retold in our words. They describe a spiritual view of illness and destiny, not medical advice. Anyone facing illness should keep working with their doctors. Inner change and good medical care belong together.
