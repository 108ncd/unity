Balance in bhakti

Love that stays true to its source

Devotion is the most natural thing in the soul, and also the easiest to bend. It can lean toward cold rules or loose sentiment, toward worldly gain or toward a love that forgets whom it loves. This page is a map of those leanings, and of the three witnesses that keep the path straight.

We describe tendencies here, not people. Every one of them can show up in any of us, in any tradition, on any given day.

Guru · sādhu · śāstra

Three witnesses

The bhakti tradition checks every step against three witnesses: the guru, the teacher who has walked the path; the sādhus, the saintly people past and present who live it; and śāstra, the revealed scripture. When all three agree, we can trust the step. When one of them is missing, imbalance creeps in.

sādhu-śāstra-guru-vākya, cittete kariyā aikya “Bring the words of the saints, the scriptures and the guru into one in your heart.” Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura, Prema-bhakti-candrikā, our rendering

Each witness guards against a different drift. Scripture alone can turn dry and argumentative. A teacher alone can turn into a personality cult. A community alone can turn into custom for its own sake. Together they correct one another.

Guru

The living teacher

Knowledge of the soul is received, not invented. We approach someone who has seen the truth, with humility, honest questions and service.

Bhagavad-gītā 4.34

Sādhu

The company of saints

“In the company of the saintly, talks of My power become a tonic for the heart and ear.” Love is caught from those who love.

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 3.25.25, our rendering

Śāstra

The scripture

“Let scripture be your measure of what should be done and what should not.” It is the shared map that keeps both teacher and student honest.

Bhagavad-gītā 16.24, our rendering

Many traditions keep a similar triple check. Islam weighs the Qur'an, the Sunnah of the Prophet and the consensus of the learned. Many Christians weigh scripture, the tradition of the Church and the living community of faith. The names differ, the wisdom is the same: no single voice should stand alone.

Śuddha and miśra · pure and mixed

Pure devotion, and what it gets mixed with

Rūpa Gosvāmī gave the classic definition of the highest bhakti. It has two edges: what it keeps out, and what it is.

anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyaṁ jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam / ānukūlyena kṛṣṇānuśīlanaṁ bhaktir uttamā “The highest devotion is free of every other desire, and not covered by the pursuit of knowledge, results or anything else. It is the loving cultivation of service to the all-attractive Personality of God [Kṛṣṇa], done in a way that pleases Him.” Rūpa Gosvāmī, Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.1.11, our rendering

When devotion is mixed with another goal, the tradition calls it miśra, “mixed”. Mixed devotion is still devotion, and the Gītā calls all who turn to God, for whatever reason, generous souls (7.16–18). It is a real stepping stone. The only risk is to mistake the step for the summit.

Yukta-vairāgya · balanced renunciation

Two ditches, one road

A road has a ditch on each side. Rūpa Gosvāmī names both. On one side is attachment: using the world for our own pleasure. On the other is a false renunciation that throws away what could be used in love.

anāsaktasya viṣayān yathārham upayuñjataḥ / nirbandhaḥ kṛṣṇa-sambandhe yuktaṁ vairāgyam ucyate “To use the things of this world as fits, without attachment, and to hold them firmly in their link with God: that is called balanced renunciation.” Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.2.255, our rendering
prāpañcikatayā buddhyā hari-sambandhi-vastunaḥ / mumukṣubhiḥ parityāgo vairāgyaṁ phalgu kathyate “When seekers of liberation reject things connected with God, thinking them merely material, that renunciation is called hollow.” Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.2.256, our rendering

The same balance applies to rules. Among the six things that weaken devotion is niyamāgraha: clinging to rules for their own sake, and also neglecting them (see Pitfalls on the path). Every imbalance on this page leans one way or the other.

The directory

Where devotion drifts

Here are the common imbalances, grouped by where they show up: in what we believe, in how we practise, in our customs and in how we live. For each we note which witness has gone quiet, what the devotion has been mixed with, and what brings it back.

In what we believe

“The form is only a step”

God's names, form, qualities and pastimes are treated as a useful picture for beginners, to be dropped once we reach the formless truth. Worship becomes a ladder kicked away at the top.

Witness lost
Śāstra, which describes the Lord's form as eternal (Gītā 7.24–25)
Mixed with
Jñāna: the goal of merging
Balance
Keep the clarity of knowledge, and let it serve love. The Gītā says the path to the unmanifest is the harder one (12.5).

Bhāgavatam 10.2.32; Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.2.256

“I am already That”

The insight that we are spirit, not body, is taken one step too far: the soul is declared identical to God, and humility before God starts to look unnecessary.

Witness lost
Guru and sādhu, who model the soul as servant
Mixed with
Jñāna
Balance
One in quality, distinct in quantity: the soul is an eternal part of God, not the whole (Gītā 15.7). See Four levels of awareness.

“All paths are the same”

The beautiful truth that many paths lead toward God is stretched into the claim that they all arrive at the same place, so the choice of path, practice or object of worship no longer matters. Discernment starts to look like intolerance.

Witness lost
Śāstra, which distinguishes goals
Mixed with
Jñāna: one formless end behind all forms
Balance
Every sincere path is a real step, and the Lord meets each seeker as they approach (Gītā 4.11). But each path reaches its own goal (Gītā 9.25). We honour every stone without calling every stone the summit.

In how we practise

Worship for what it brings

Prayer and ritual are used to get things: money, power, a partner, victory over rivals. The deity becomes a supplier, and worship stops when the wish is granted.

Witness lost
Śāstra, which says such fruit is temporary (Gītā 7.20–23)
Mixed with
Karma: desire for results
Balance
Ask, but let the asking grow into love. The Gītā honours even those who come in need (7.16) and invites them further.

Practice outside scripture

Rites are invented or borrowed without regard for scripture: practices of intoxication, sexuality or sacrifice that are called spiritual because they produce strong experiences.

Witness lost
Śāstra
Mixed with
Karma, often in the mode of darkness (Gītā 17.4–6)
Balance
“One who sets scripture aside and acts on impulse reaches neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the supreme goal” (Gītā 16.23).

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.2.101

Power and technique

Practice becomes a technology for peace of mind, energy or special abilities. Visions and inner powers are collected like trophies.

Witness lost
Sādhu: the saints ask for nothing but service
Mixed with
Yoga
Balance
Keep the discipline, offer the fruit. The summit of yoga is the yogī absorbed in love (Gītā 6.47).

Bhāgavatam 11.15.33

Imitating the summit

The highest states of love described in scripture are acted out before the heart is ready. Tears and ecstasy are performed, the intimate pastimes of God are discussed casually, and the long work of purification is skipped.

Witness lost
Guru and śāstra, which describe the stages in order
Mixed with
Sense desire dressed as love
Balance
Don't skip steps. Love grows from faith, good company and steady practice, stage by stage (Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.4.15–16).

In our customs

Authority by birth

The right to teach or to be respected is claimed by family, caste or lineage of blood, rather than by realisation and character.

Witness lost
Śāstra and sādhu
Mixed with
Karma: social status
Balance
“Whether a scholar, a renunciant or a labourer, whoever knows the truth of God is a guru” (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 8.128).

Rules above love

Ritual purity and correct procedure become the whole of religion. Outward correctness is policed while kindness and devotion are forgotten.

Witness lost
Sādhu: the saints put love first
Mixed with
Karma: ritual for merit
Balance
Rules serve remembrance of God, never the other way round (Padma Purāṇa, quoted in Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.2.8).

The outer dress

Spiritual identity is put on from the outside: robes, ornaments and roles that belong to an inner state. Sometimes a person even dresses as God or as one of His eternal companions.

Witness lost
Guru: the spiritual identity is revealed within, not costumed
Mixed with
Pride and display
Balance
The inner life matters most. Outer signs are a help when they come from it.

“Only our way”

Loyalty to one's own teacher and community hardens into the belief that it is the only true path, and sincere seekers outside it are looked down on.

Witness lost
Sādhu: the saints honour devotion wherever it is found
Mixed with
Pride of belonging
Balance
Be firm in your own path and humble toward all: “humbler than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree, giving respect to everyone” (Śikṣāṣṭaka 3).

In how we live

Sense pleasure called spirituality

Lust and indulgence are given spiritual names. Bodily intimacy is described as divine love, and self-control is dismissed as repression.

Witness lost
All three
Mixed with
Kāma: desire for one's own pleasure
Balance
Prema is the wish to please God; kāma is the wish to please oneself. The two are as different as gold and iron (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Ādi 4.164–165).

The teacher above the teaching

The guru is worshipped as God in a way that cuts him or her off from scripture and the wider community of saints. Nothing the teacher says can be questioned.

Witness lost
Śāstra and sādhu, which are meant to confirm the guru
Mixed with
Personality cult
Balance
Honour the teacher as the messenger of God. A true teacher points beyond themselves, and welcomes the check of scripture.

Going beyond one's teacher

A student claims to have surpassed the teacher and the tradition, and starts a new version of the teaching with themselves at the centre.

Witness lost
Guru
Mixed with
Ambition
Balance
Real growth deepens gratitude to those who came before.

Endless accumulation

Work becomes the restless pursuit of more, regardless of the harm it does to people, animals and the earth. This is what the Gītā calls ugra-karma, fierce and violent work, and it falls into vikarma, action against the order of nature and of God.

Witness lost
Śāstra: everything belongs to the Lord
Mixed with
Lobha: greed
Balance
“Everything here is pervaded by the Lord. Enjoy what is given to you, and do not covet what belongs to another” (Īśopaniṣad 1).

Bhagavad-gītā 3.12, 4.17, 16.9, 16.13–15

Service without the root

Serving people is declared to be the same as serving God, and social work quietly takes the place of devotion. The compassion is real, but God Himself drops out of the picture, and the help reaches the body but not the soul.

Witness lost
Śāstra, which gives the order: God first, and all beings through Him
Mixed with
Karma: humanitarian work for its own sake
Balance
“As watering the root of a tree nourishes its trunk, branches and leaves, so worship of the Lord satisfies everyone” (Bhāgavatam 4.31.14). Serve people as an offering to God, and share with them what feeds the soul too.

Violence on the plate

Food is chosen only for taste or strength, with no thought for the animals who suffer for it or for offering it first to God. Spiritual life and the kitchen are kept in separate rooms.

Witness lost
Śāstra and sādhu: ahiṁsā is honoured by saints of every tradition
Mixed with
Sense desire, in the modes of passion and darkness (Gītā 17.8–10)
Balance
“Offer Me with love a leaf, a flower, fruit or water, and I accept it” (Gītā 9.26). Those who cook only for themselves “eat sin” (Gītā 3.13). Protecting the cow is named a natural duty (Gītā 18.44).

A word of caution

Look in the mirror first

A map of imbalances can become a new imbalance: a list for judging others. The tradition warns that contempt for those who love God is the gravest offence of all, the mad elephant that tramples the creeper of devotion (Pitfalls on the path). The point of this page is to check our own heart, and to honour every sincere seeker wherever they stand.

para-svabhāva-karmāṇi na praśaṁsen na garhayet “Neither praise nor condemn the nature and actions of others.” Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.28.1, our rendering
